I have been dabbling in game development for much of my life, however only recently did I have a concept I felt would sell. This has led me to trying to learn more about how indie dev functions, and that led me to a talk by Grey Alien Games, who as of the 2016 date of the talk, had been making games for over a decade. 

This talk was interesting because it served as a look into an indie developer operating as a business more than an artist. Ironically, it also looked like the kinds of games I always call soulless products instead of passion projects. However, this developer eventually came to make Forbidden Solitaire, which is not just a hit but a highly interesting and artistic game in my eyes, so perhaps I have misjudged his prior works.

Onto the contents of the talk and my thoughts though.

It did feel there was some good emphasis on getting your first game out. Scope creep is most likely to burn you out here, so avoiding the loss of momentum and getting something small out is a great boon to your survivability. From another video I have been digesting in my head - [# I Analysed 62,000 Indie Developers. Here's What Actually Predicts Success](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WueQ75GP1wc) - Most successful devs have more than 5 games under their belt by the time that they get a hit. This is true, but not the sum of this picture I don't think. 

There are definite benefits to releasing several games consistently. There are learnings that come from this process, and you likely aren't tripping over the same twigs twice. You'll be able to build bigger in less time, and things that took your whole attention in game one are basic in game 6. However, something I didn't realize was the compounding effect of having several games out.

With multiple games out and available, sales may slow down on older ones but not stop. This means that while there is a logarithmic scale to how much you make, when you have several games on that timeline, it can add up to something significant. On top of this, some games blow up years after release (see Fear and Hunger, which originally released at the end of 2018, but didn't see popularity until 2023, 5 years later). Grey Alien Games added a similar sentiment, saying 5 years after some of his "bonus" match 3 games, they blew up and made his investment back. This compounding income has a similar additive effect to interest, but not necessarily compounding in the way that makes interest valuable. This came at a big cost at times. He often was struggling for money and looking for funding. He also notes he took contract work to make ends meet. This is not a short-term plan, nor is it sustainable alone. It takes time to set up and time to mature, if it does at all. But the longer you can stay in the ring, the more likely you are to win, and that is another sentiment that was echoed in the video mentioned prior.

I think it's important to note that there is some survivorship bias influencing this talk. He made it long enough to see the later bounties of these games. But if you aren't able to keep making games, or starve before they make money back, it doesn't matter if you have 20 out. You need to survive in the meantime. His games before Forbidden Solitaire either didn't make much, or only made their money years later, so you have to be prepared to wait for a return on your investment. This only works if you can stick around.

In summary, it is an enlightening talk. Much of the talk is his story, so there weren't many hard skills or lessons to draw from, however what learnings he did share I feel are quite vital to understanding the way the market functions for those who aren't lucky enough to get a hit. Game development is a creative process, and thus its already likely those who make it are also those who cannot live doing anything else, but if you are one of those people and are able to live through the hard startup, you are more likely than most to make it into royalty income, and through building that up you may see a comfortable life. 

In the end though, this relies less on if you are capable of making good games, and more on if you can survive the process.
